The history of the railway in Komló dates back to the end of the nineteenth century. The construction of the line began in eighteen ninety-six, and it was opened on May thirteenth, eighteen ninety-seven. The most important reason for creating the railway was coal mining in Komló: a connection had to be established between the mines and the Budapest–Pécs main line so that the extracted coal could be transported by rail.
The first station building in Komló was completed by the time of the inauguration. According to old sources, a second-class local railway  reception building stood in Komló, which clearly shows that the line originally started as a local interest railway, but quickly gained an important role due to mining.
The railway did not only serve passenger traffic. Before Komló, there was a freight station called Komló-Tahhair, as well as connections to the coal washery and the mining railway network. These operated between nineteen fifty-two and two thousand, but with the decline of mining, they ceased operations and were demolished around two thousand five.
The history of passenger transport has seen several turns. On December thirteenth, two thousand nine, passenger traffic on the Komló line was discontinued. A year later, in December two thousand ten, it was restarted, with trains running mainly between Sásd and Komló. Later, the traffic became increasingly sparse, and on August first, two thousand twenty-three, passenger transport stopped once again.
However, the history of line forty-seven has not ended. From August first, twenty twenty-six, passenger railway service will restart between Dombóvár and Komló. The trains will run under the designation S four hundred forty-seven, using Bz railcars, completing the journey between Komló and Dombóvár in about forty-two minutes.
Thus, Komló railway station is not simply a terminus. It is the end point of a line brought to life by coal mining, closed and reopened several times, which remains an important part of the city’s transport and railway heritage today.
