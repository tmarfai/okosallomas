The history of the railway in Kom-loh dates back to the end of the nineteenth century. The construction of the line began in eighteen ninety-six, and it was opened on May thirteenth, eighteen ninety-seven. The most important reason for creating the railway was coal mining in Kom-loh: a connection had to be established between the mines and the Budapest–Pécs main line so that the extracted coal could be transported by rail.
The railway did not only serve passenger traffic. Before Kom-loh, there was a freight station called Kom-loh Tahhair, as well as connections to the coal washery and the mining railway network. These operated between nineteen fifty-two and two thousand, but with the decline of mining, they ceased operations and were demolished around two thousand five.
In the history of the Kom-loh railway line, regional transport continued to play an important role even after the decline of mining. For a long time, the line connected Kom-loh to the main railway network and remained a symbolic element of the city's transport links.
On August first, two thousand twenty-three, passenger transport stopped on the line leading to Kom-loh. However, the history of line forty-seven reaches a new chapter in twenty twenty-six: from August first, twenty twenty-six, passenger railway service will restart between Dom-boh-vahr and Kom-loh. The trains will run under the designation S-four-hundred-forty-seven, using Bee-Zet railcars, completing the journey between Kom-loh and Dom-boh-vahr in about forty-two minutes.
Thus, Kom-loh railway station is not simply a terminus. It is the end point of a line brought to life by coal mining, which remains an important part of the city’s transport and railway heritage today.
